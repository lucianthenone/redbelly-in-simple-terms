## Facts, as said in the video

### F01. You can send a photo around the world in a second.

Everyday comparison to set up the contrast. Not a claim about Redbelly.

- No source needed: everyday comparison, not a statement about Redbelly.

### F02. Buying into a fund can still take two business days to settle.

Today's typical settlement window for listed funds, as described by Tokeniser.

- S5: "Every subscription, redemption and transfer remains unsettled for two business days in listed funds, and often longer in private assets."

### F03. The money and the record of who owns what sit in different places, and the copies have to be checked against each other.

How settlement works today: cash and ownership records move separately and must be reconciled.

- S5: "Today, cash and units move separately, and records are updated days later."
- S5: "When the trade and the register update together, reconciliation is no longer required and settlement breaks are reduced."
- S8: "The intricate payment waterfalls, inter-system reconciliations, and multi-party dependencies have traditionally limited transparency and liquidity."

### F04. Redbelly is a public network.

Described as public by Tokeniser and as public in the RBA report.

- S4: "Is Redbelly a private network? No. Redbelly is a public network ..."
- S9, p. 12: "Pilot use cases were conducted on a range of public and private ... platforms, including ... Redbelly Network (public) ..."

### F05. Redbelly was built in Australia and grew out of research at the University of Sydney and CSIRO, Australia's national science agency.

Origin stated by Redbelly and Tokeniser; joint venture listed in Redbelly's own timeline.

- S3: "Developed at the University of Sydney and CSIRO, Redbelly Network's homegrown ..."
- S4: "Redbelly was born from research at the University of Sydney and CSIRO, Australia's government science agency, with a patented consensus method built from scratch."
- S7: "CSIRO ... Australia's national science agency worked alongside that research to take it from published papers to a network that institutions can run in production, under Australian governance."
- S11: "Block8, CSIRO and University of Sydney create Redbelly Network as a joint venture with seed funding"

### F06. Redbelly brings the money and the ownership records together in one place.

Redbelly describes its design as joining payments and asset records into a single system, which reduces reconciliation.

- S8: "Its deterministic consensus unifies payments and asset ... into a single system, reducing reconciliation and enabling collateral mobility."
- S1: "Establish digital registries as a shared source of truth across issuers, managers, and investors."

### F07. Payment and ownership move in one step. If either side fails, nothing moves.

Tokeniser, which runs on Redbelly, exchanges cash and units in a single step or not at all.

- S5: "On Tokeniser, cash and units are exchanged in a single step and the register updates simultaneously, or the transaction does not proceed."
- S4: "Tokeniser gives you a familiar platform. Underneath it runs Redbelly Network ..."

### F08. Once it's done, it's final.

Deterministic finality: a confirmed transaction cannot be reversed.

- S2: "Finality - Deterministic finality ensures transactions are confirmed once and cannot be reversed, providing certainty for financial markets"
- S4: "Deterministic finality settles a transaction in a single round, final the moment it completes."

### F09. The people trading are verified.

Redbelly describes a network of verified participants, with verifiable credentials gating every holder.

- S1: "A network of clean liquidity and verified participants enables structured products with clearly defined investor eligibility."
- S2: "Accountability - Verified participants enable full auditability and clear accountability across all transactions"
- S4: "Verifiable credentials gate every holder, enabling direct-to-investor distribution within your mandate."

### F10. Their private details stay protected.

Identity is verified while privacy is protected with zero-knowledge proofs.

- S3: "Participants verify their identities, but their privacy remains protected through zero-knowledge proofs."
- S12: "All users are verified using digital identities and zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP), ensuring compliance without compromising personal privacy."

### F11. The rules are built in. Before a trade goes through, the system checks the buyer is allowed to buy it. If not, it doesn't happen.

Eligibility is enforced at the point of trade; trades that fail a check do not proceed.

- S2: "A network of clean liquidity, combined with verifiable credentials enable tokenised investment products with defined investor eligibility, enforced at the point of trade."
- S6: "Before any trade proceeds, the investor's identity, eligibility (including wholesale or sophisticated status where required) and your limits are verified automatically. Trades that fail a check do not proceed, and every check is logged."

### F12. Things like property, loans, bonds and investment funds can be issued and traded digitally.

Redbelly lists these asset classes; the RBA report records funds, bonds and loan-backed securities piloted on Redbelly.

- S1: "Treasuries & Bonds, Private Credit, Carbon Credits, Derivatives & Commodities, Insurance, Real Estate"
- S1: "Redbelly provides the infrastructure to issue, manage, and distribute regulated digital financial assets to a global investor base."
- S9, p. 61: "Macropod piloted two use cases. The first use case tested issuance, trading and redemption of tokenised units in a wholesale managed investment scheme ... The second use case tested a tokenised corporate bond ... used in use cases: Redbelly Network."
- S9, p. 61: "NotCentralised piloted the structuring, issuance and management of a tokenised structured finance security backed by loans ... used in use case: Redbelly Network."

### F13. They settle almost instantly.

Settlement is described as quasi-instant, moving from two days (T+2) to the same day (T+0).

- S4: "Settlement is quasi-instant. Deterministic consensus finalises a transaction in a single round, with finality the moment it completes."
- S5: "Move from T+2 to T+0 settlement."

### F14. People call this tokenisation: issuing and trading a real asset as a digital version.

Plain-language definition, consistent with how Redbelly and the Acacia pilot describe a digital twin of a real asset.

- S2: "Asset issuers can issue and distribute products globally, with compliance built into the infrastructure"
- S8: "... the consortium is mirroring a live digital twin of the real-world asset, the Wisr Freedom Trust 2025-1 ..."

### F15. In Project Acacia, the Reserve Bank of Australia trialled a pilot digital form of its own money.

The RBA and the Digital Finance CRC ran Project Acacia and issued a pilot wholesale central bank digital currency (wCBDC).

- S10: "The use cases also explored multiple methods for settling tokenised asset transactions using different forms of public and private digital money, including traditional RBA exchange settlement account (ESA) balances, a pilot wholesale central bank digital currency (wCBDC), tokenised commercial bank deposits and stablecoins."
- S9, p. 13: "The total value of pilot wCBDC issuance over the project was $4.4 million with individual transaction values of up to $250,000 seen in use cases."

### F16. Redbelly was one of three platforms that carried it.

The RBA final report names three platforms used with pilot wCBDC: AP+ HashSphere, Canvas Connect and Redbelly Network.

- S9, p. 13: "Use cases used three of the short-listed ... platforms for their experimentation with pilot wCBDC: Australian Payments Plus's (AP+) HashSphere instance ..., Canvas Connect ..., and Redbelly Network ..."
